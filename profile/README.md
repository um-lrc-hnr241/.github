# Honors 241 Project Sites

This organization hosts the team portfolio websites for **Honors 241:
Philosophy of Mind through Westworld** at the University of Michigan
(Fall 2026). Each repository here is one student team's portfolio, published
with GitHub Pages.

## About the course

Honors 241, taught by Professor George Hoffmann, explores the nature of consciousness through classical and
contemporary works of philosophy, from Socrates to cyborgs. Taking the HBO
series *Westworld* and its speculation about humans' robotic nature as a
starting point, the course examines attention, memory, and the question of
free will, and asks how philosophical works can respond to the challenges
raised by new technology. Readings range from Plato, Augustine, and Descartes
to Hegel, Fanon, and Elaine Scarry.

It is also a first-year writing course, and a team-based one. Students work
in the same team all semester. Each team takes on one difficult reading and
studies it closely, and each student writes a series of essays on the team's
topic, revised through rounds of peer review, instructor feedback, and
in-class workshops.

## Why these sites exist

The team portfolio is where that semester of writing is published. Each
portfolio brings together:

- every team member's individual essays, including close engagement with a
  passage from the team's reading, a thematic analysis, a response to an
  objection, a compare-and-contrast essay, a personal evocation, and a
  reflective conclusion
- an introduction and a conclusion written collectively by the team
- the team's own design and illustrations

The portfolios are public by design and stay open after the course ends.
Writing for readers beyond the classroom is part of the assignment. Because
the portfolios include personal writing, some students sign their work with
an alias.

On the final day of class, the whole course tours the sites in a gallery walk
and votes on them, and the section instructors award a jury prize.

## How the sites are built

The writing, the structure, and the look of each portfolio come from the
team. To turn those into a working website without requiring anyone to know
how to code, each team keeps a shared planning document with five sections:

| Section | What goes in it |
| --- | --- |
| Requirements | The site's purpose, audience, and constraints |
| Sitemap | Each page and what it is for |
| Content | The actual text for every page |
| Design & Style Guide | Colors, fonts, layout, and reference material |
| Decisions Log | A dated record of what the team agreed on |

Teams direct an AI coding agent by leaving comments in that document. They
can ask it for advice, ask it to build or change the site from their plan, or
roll back a change they don't like. The agent commits the resulting HTML and
CSS to the team's repository here, and GitHub Pages publishes it.

## Finding a portfolio

Each repository is named for its team — the teams are named after
*Westworld* characters — and is published at:

```
https://um-lrc-hnr241.github.io/<repository-name>/
```
